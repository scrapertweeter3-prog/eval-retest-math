One prompt edit re-runs the whole suite

A production agent ships with a 500 case eval suite, each case a full task replay averaging 45,000 input tokens and 3,000 output tokens. At $3.00 a million in and $15.00 a million out, one case costs $0.18 and one full regression run costs $90. The default policy runs the suite after every prompt edit, and at four edits a week that is 208 runs, $18,720 a year, most of it spent re-proving prompts the edit never touched.

A dependency graph prices the alternative. Mark which cases actually reach the edited prompt and 90 of the 500 do; a targeted run costs $16.20, $3,369.60 across the year, and the difference is $15,350.40, an 82 percent cut. Building the graph took eight engineer hours, $480 once, and at $295 a week in savings it pays for itself before the third week.

The graph's precision is the whole product. A case wrongly skipped that would have caught a regression turns into a six hour fix at $360, which wipes 1.2 weeks of savings; two careless exclusions a quarter and the graph loses money while feeling efficient. The audit question is not whether the graph shrinks the run. It is how often a skipped case would have been the one that caught it.
